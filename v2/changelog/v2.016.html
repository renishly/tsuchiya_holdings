How a coefficient is fitted and approved

  • The question: across the listed housebuilders and years, how much did operating profit change (as a share of revenue) when order intake grew, or when timber prices rose? Order figures come from each annual report's 受注実績 table and are used only if they are 30–300% of that year's revenue, so misread units are excluded.
  • The test, fixed in advance as for the M1 weights: the expected sign (more orders → more profit; higher timber prices → less profit), t ≥ 2, at least 15 observations.
  • Your advance approval (30 Oct 2026, recorded in state/approvals.md): a coefficient that passes is applied without a new approval request; one that fails is 0. It covers order momentum, supply costs and land prices. M1 settings and method weights still need your approval each time.
  • The yen amount is the slope × Tsuchiya's revenue × 1%: ¥34.6m of operating profit for each 1% of order growth. A 10% fall in orders since the forecast, with the whole year left, would raise Md by about ¥346m.
